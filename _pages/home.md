# Ní Leathlobhair Lab

We are a multidisciplinary research group in the School of Genetics and Microbiology at [Trinity College Dublin](https://www.tcd.ie/). We investigate how genomes evolve, with a particular focus on the origins, patterns and consequences of ploidy change in human cancers.

A central aim is to map the full spectrum of chromosome gains, losses and whole-genome duplication across cancers, determine the mutational and selective processes shaping these changes, and understand their consequences for tumour evolution and patient outcomes. We use large-scale whole-genome sequencing datasets, computational methods and quantitative evolutionary approaches to reconstruct these histories.

We also study the relationship between development and cancer. Work on testicular germ cell tumours, gestational trophoblastic disease and malignancy arising *in utero* explores how developmental context shapes somatic evolution—and what cancer genomes can reveal about early human development.

<div class="lab-home-copy" markdown="1">

We are interested in the evolution of endogenous retroviruses and other endogenous viral elements, including their distribution and persistence across host genomes.

A separate line of research examines the evolution of histone mRNA 3′-end processing across eukaryotes, including how components of this specialised pathway have been conserved, modified or lost.

We develop and apply computational and comparative genomic approaches, and are interested in how emerging genomic datasets can help resolve evolutionary histories at finer scales.
